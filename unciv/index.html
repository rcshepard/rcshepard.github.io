Chronicle

    How Hinterlands works

    You start with one settler and one warrior, at peace with a rival realm across the map. Wipe out every rival unit and city to win.

    Peace and war

    • Realms start at peace. At peace, nobody can attack, capture or occupy anything, and units keep out of each other's borders.
    • War starts if you enter a rival's territory, attack them, or declare war yourself. The game asks before any of these.
    • Units of two realms standing side by side for more than 5 turns risk a border incident. From then on a d6 is rolled every turn they stay, and a 1 means war.
    • The AI declares war on its own only when it's overwhelmingly ahead: 10 times your military, or borders covering most of the map's land.
    • If a rival founds a city or buys land where your unit stands while you're at peace, your unit moves back to the nearest neutral ground.
    • After 10 turns of war, either side can offer peace, with up to its whole treasury as compensation. The AI uses its war test: while it's 10 times stronger or holds most of the land, it won't talk. Otherwise it wants gold in proportion to how much bigger its army is, and tells you its price if it turns you down. A losing AI may offer you peace and gold.
    • A peace treaty lasts at least 40 turns. During it nobody can declare war, border incidents can't happen, and units can't cross each other's borders. Units go home when it's signed.

    Moving and fighting

    • Each unit gets one move per turn in any of the eight directions. Water is fine. Entering mountains ends the move and costs the next turn too.
    • Moving between two road, railroad or city squares costs half a move. The streets drawn between neighboring urban squares don't speed anything up.
    • Moving between two of your own railroad squares, or between a railroad square and your city center, costs nothing. A big city laced with railroads can move units across it in a single turn.
    • Moving into an enemy unit starts a fight: d6 plus attack against d6 plus defense. Higher total wins and the loser is destroyed. A tie means nobody gives ground.
    • Units defending inside a city or district add that square's population plus their neighbors'. An undefended city or district fights back with the same bonus.
    • An attacker fighting inside the defender's city ranges has its total cut by 20%.
    • Land military units can spend 2 turns building fortifications. A fortified defender's whole total is multiplied by 1.5, on top of any cut to the attacker. Moving or attacking leaves the fortifications behind.
    • Settlers defend with 1 plus their roll and nothing else, and a tie counts against them.
    • Ships stay on water. They fight like warriors, but at five times strength against land units on water.
    • Once your realm reaches 100 people in all, cities train infantry (10 attack, 10 defense) instead of warriors, archers and spearmen, and those can be upgraded to infantry for 100 gold each. At 150 people cities can also build tanks (30 attack, 20 defense), which can't be made by upgrading.
    • Damaged units fight weaker, in step with their health. Units heal 10% a turn only inside their own territory.

    Capturing and occupying

    • Capturing a city takes its districts and the improvements in its range too. For 50 turns it's under your control at 20 gold a turn; if you can't pay, it revolts back to its old owner. After that it's fully yours.
    • At war, a unit that ends its turn on a farm, road, mine or district the enemy built is occupying it, and costs 10 gold a turn instead of 1. If you can't pay, or choose not to, the unit loses 10% health a turn instead.

    Settlers and land

    • Settlers never get used up. They work for a number of turns: city 5, district 5, harbor 5, mine 5, farm 3, road 2. Moving them cancels the work.
    • City centers must be at least 5 squares apart, and a new city can't touch another urban square. Building next to your own urban square makes a district instead, which belongs to the nearest of your cities. Districts go inside your own borders.
    • Each city claims 3 squares in every direction for free. You can buy squares touching its border, out to 5 squares, for 50 gold each. Rivals can't put farms, districts, mines or harbors inside your borders.
    • A road built through a district is a railroad. Past 5 squares, and out to 8, a city can only buy squares next to its railroads, and districts there must sit on a road or next to a railroad.
    • Harbors go on a water square directly beside one of your land urban squares.
    • About 5% of mountains hold gold, 5% coal and 5% iron. A mine on one earns 25 gold a turn once a road or urban square links it to a city, industrial district or harbor. Each deposit lasts 200 earning turns.

    Links

    • Two of your cities linked by railroads through districts, with roads across open land where rails can't go, earn the two whole cities' population divided by 3, every turn. A district square without a railroad breaks the line. Every linked pair counts.
    • A city linked by road to a foreign city while the two realms are at peace earns both realms the two cities' combined population every turn. Streets through urban squares count as road here. Each city can trade with one foreign city, its nearest linked one.
    • Enemy units standing on a link cut it until they leave.

    Demand

    • Each city has a demand meter for residential, commercial and industrial, like SimCity's.
    • Residential: residential districts only grow while the city has more jobs than residents. Each farm feeding the city makes 2 jobs, each commercial or industrial district 3, and each harbor 1.
    • Commercial: every 6 residents, center included, support one commercial district. Harbors count as commercial.
    • Industrial: each commercial district needs half an industrial district, plus one more for every 12 residents.
    • Build more commercial or industry than the city can use, and each one works at reduced strength.

    City projects

    • Instead of a unit, a city can spend 40 turns on an upgrade project. Each can be built once per city, and switching away keeps its progress.
    • Apartment blocks need 4 residential districts. Afterwards the city's residential districts can grow to 10. Residents past 6 on a square need jobs but no farms.
    • Heavy industry needs an industrial district with a railroad. Afterwards every industrial district with a railroad gains 3 jobs and earns 3 gold a turn.
    • Department stores need a commercial district with a railroad. Afterwards every commercial district with a railroad gains 3 jobs and earns 3 gold a turn.

    Growth and gold

    • Cities grow by one every 10 turns, residential districts every 20 while there's demand, both up to 6. A city's badge shows the population of the whole city.
    • Any square above size 3 needs food from a farm for each extra citizen. A farm feeds a city within 2 squares of it or its districts. Farms on grassland grow twice the food, enough for 2 citizens, but every farm makes the same 2 jobs. Farms can't go on mountains or frozen land.
    • Cities and residential districts earn gold equal to their population. Commercial districts and harbors earn 10% of neighboring population, doubled when linked to their city and scaled down when there's too much commercial.
    • A city trains a unit in 20 turns minus its size. Each neighboring industrial district cuts that by 25%, doubled when linked. Neighboring urban squares count as linked, and roads link the rest.
    • Every unit and road square costs 1 gold a turn. Your starting settler and warrior are free. If the treasury runs dry, your newest unit is disbanded, then roads fall apart.
